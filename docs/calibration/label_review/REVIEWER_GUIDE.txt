REVIEWER GUIDE: SQUAT AND PUSH-UP FORM LABELS
Version 1 | 20 November 2026

PURPOSE
Record what a human reviewer can observe in each repetition, independently of
the app. These annotations will help evaluate rep-specific form feedback.
The numbered review pack contains 98 side-view videos. A contact sheet is a
preview; it does not contain enough movement to label a repetition by itself.

FILES TO USE
- outputs/form_label_review/reviewer_index.csv: video list and review progress.
- outputs/form_label_review/review_###.mp4: full videos to watch.
- outputs/form_label_review/review_###.jpg: quick visual previews.
- outputs/form_label_review/reviewer_rep_annotations.csv: your labeling sheet.
Open the CSVs in a spreadsheet program and save them back as UTF-8 CSV.
Keep the existing column names. Use one row per observed repetition cycle.

BEFORE YOU BEGIN
1. Make a personal copy of the blank annotation sheet, named with your reviewer
   ID, for example reviewer_rep_annotations_R01.csv. Keep the master blank.
2. Use an anonymous, consistent reviewer ID. Record your actual qualification;
   for example PE instructor, certified trainer, or student volunteer. A volunteer
   review must not be presented as an expert assessment.
3. Keep source good/bad labels, source filenames, private ID mappings and app
   predictions hidden while reviewing. Work only from the numbered review pack.
4. With the review lead, agree on the exercise variants, expected movement range
   and criteria below. Write these in a separate rubric_decisions.txt before
   labeling. An unspecified target means uncertain, not an invented threshold.
5. Practice on a small shared development subset. Resolve differences in the
   rubric, then freeze its version before the main review. Record any revisions.

RUBRIC DECISIONS TO WRITE DOWN
- Rubric version, date, review lead and reviewer IDs.
- Accepted squat and push-up variants; how to handle unsupported variants.
- What visibly meets the depth target for each supported variant.
- What visibly meets the return/top target for each supported variant.
- What counts as a visible trunk alignment issue for each supported variant.
- Which categories are not applicable to a supported variant, and why.
- Whether overall_form covers only these three faults or additional criteria.
  If additional criteria are used, list them and describe their evidence in notes.
Do not use the app's learned thresholds as the human reference standard.

REVIEW EACH VIDEO
1. Find its review_id and exercise in reviewer_index.csv. Mark its review_status
   in_progress in your working copy of the index.
2. Watch the whole video once at normal speed. Confirm the exercise and view.
   Use slow playback or frame stepping to inspect ambiguous moments afterward.
3. Identify each discernible movement cycle independently of the app's count:
   Squat: top position -> descent -> lowest position -> return toward the top.
   Push-up: top position -> lowering -> lowest position -> return toward the top.
   A cycle can finish with incomplete extension; retain it and label that fault
   if the agreed rubric supports it. Otherwise such errors would be excluded.
4. Mark start_seconds immediately before descent/lowering begins. Mark
   end_seconds at the end of the return attempt, before the next descent.
   Use seconds from the ORIGINAL full video, never from the start of a preview
   or cropped clip. A frame timestamp is frame_index / fps, starting at frame 0.
   Be consistent about boundaries; consecutive cycles may share an endpoint.
5. Add one row for each cycle, numbered rep_index 1, 2, 3, ... within that video.
   Do not create repetitions for standing still, setup, or a contact-sheet image.
6. Beginning/end fragments that omit part of a cycle are not complete cycle
   references. Record their time ranges and the reason for exclusion in a
   separate review_notes.txt, so they do not disappear without explanation.
7. Judge the three fault categories and overall_form using the agreed rubric.
   Record what you saw, including relevant timestamps, in evidence_notes.
8. After all cycles are reviewed, mark review_status complete in your index.
   If no cycle can be identified, record that video and the reason in
   review_notes.txt, then mark it complete. Do not manufacture a placeholder rep.

EXACT VALUES FOR THE ANNOTATION COLUMNS
review_id               Copy exactly, for example review_002.
exercise                squat or pushup; verify against the actual movement.
rep_index               Positive integer; unique within a video and reviewer.
start_seconds           Nonnegative number, relative to the full video.
end_seconds             Greater than start_seconds; within the video duration.
view                    side for supported side-view recordings. If unsuitable,
                        describe the actual view/problem in evidence_notes.
assessable              yes or no.
unassessable_reason     Blank for yes. For no, explain missing visibility,
                        obstruction, unsupported view/variant, or other reason.
overall_form            correct, incorrect, uncertain, or unassessable.
limited_depth           present, absent, uncertain, or not_applicable.
incomplete_extension    present, absent, uncertain, or not_applicable.
trunk_alignment_issue   present, absent, uncertain, or not_applicable.
evidence_notes          Specific visible evidence and timestamps; no app scores.
reviewer_id             Your consistent anonymous ID, for example R01.
reviewer_qualification  Your actual role or qualification.
reviewed_at             ISO date/time with timezone, for example
                        2026-11-20T16:00:00+08:00 for Philippine time.
second_reviewer_id      Blank during an independent first review.
adjudication_status     Blank during a first review. Use agreed, resolved, or
                        unresolved only after comparing independent reviews.

HOW TO CHOOSE THE FAULT LABELS
limited_depth:
  present: the visible movement falls short of the agreed depth target.
  absent: sufficient visual evidence shows that target was reached.
  uncertain: the target is unspecified or the relevant evidence is ambiguous.
incomplete_extension:
  present: the return attempt visibly falls short of the agreed top target.
  absent: sufficient visual evidence shows that target was reached.
  uncertain: the endpoint is hidden, ambiguous, or cut off.
trunk_alignment_issue:
  present: a deviation defined by the agreed exercise-specific rubric is visible.
  absent: adequate visible evidence shows no defined deviation during the cycle.
  uncertain: the view or clothing does not support a confident judgment.
Use not_applicable only when the written rubric makes a category inapplicable
to an accepted variant. Missing evidence calls for uncertain, not not_applicable.
Do not label every forward lean or every small angle difference as an error.

HOW TO CHOOSE OVERALL_FORM
- correct: the required criteria in the written rubric were assessable and met.
- incorrect: at least one required criterion was visibly violated; describe it.
- uncertain: some evidence is usable but the overall judgment is ambiguous.
- unassessable: the recording does not support an overall judgment; set
  assessable=no and fill unassessable_reason. Individual fault fields may retain
  observable evidence; mark hidden/ambiguous faults uncertain.
A missing pose or an app tracking failure is not evidence of incorrect form.
Assessable=yes may still have an uncertain fault or overall label. Do not force
an answer to fill the sheet. Leave a row's labels blank only while review is pending.

ILLUSTRATIVE NOTES ONLY -- THESE ARE NOT LABELS FOR THE ACTUAL VIDEOS
Example A: "At 4.20 s the agreed depth target is visibly not reached; the return
target is reached at 5.10 s." This could support limited_depth=present and
incomplete_extension=absent after viewing the entire cycle under the rubric.
Example B: "Hip is obscured from 8.10 to 8.90 s; alignment cannot be judged."
Use uncertain for the affected fault. Do not turn it into an incorrect label.

SECOND REVIEW AND DISAGREEMENTS
The second reviewer labels a separate sheet without seeing the first review.
Match rows by review_id and time interval; rep_index may differ if reviewers
identified different boundaries. Resolve cycle-count/boundary differences first.
Preserve both original sheets. Put consensus decisions in a separate sheet.
Record both reviewer IDs and whether each decision was agreed, resolved after
discussion, or remains unresolved. Retain unresolved cases in the audit.
If the rubric changes, record the change and re-review affected rows consistently.

BEFORE HANDING THE SHEET BACK
- IDs match the index; exercise names and label spellings use the values above.
- All times are numeric and within the full video's duration.
- No duplicate (review_id, rep_index, reviewer_id) rows or overlapping cycles
  without an explanation in review_notes.txt.
- Every completed row identifies its reviewer, qualification and review time.
- Every assessable=no row has an overall_form of unassessable and a reason.
- Every incorrect judgment identifies the violated criterion in evidence_notes.
- All excluded fragments and videos without identifiable cycles are accounted for.
- The CSV still has the original columns; notes containing commas are quoted
  by the spreadsheet's CSV exporter.
Return your annotation sheet, working index, review_notes.txt and rubric decisions.

WHAT THIS REVIEW CAN ESTABLISH
These labels can support development and evaluation of specific visible faults.
They do not establish medical safety or every aspect of exercise technique.
These participants have prior project exposure; relabeling them does not create
a fresh final test. Reserve new participants before tuning and final evaluation.
